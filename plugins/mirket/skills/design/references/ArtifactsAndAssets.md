# Visual artifacts and assets

Use for illustration, diagrams, icons, marks, posters, banners, slide decks and other visual deliverables. Design owns the visual direction; use the appropriate production workflow for the requested medium.

## Start from where it will be seen

Determine what the audience must understand or feel, where they see the work, the available size or time, and what they need to do next. Identify final and editable formats, aspect ratios, safe regions, accessibility and production constraints. Check current printer or platform specifications when they matter. Collect approved copy, marks, data and imagery. Keep permitted use, real evidence and placeholders distinct.

Select one dominant element for the composition or frame. Sketch the eye path, important negative space, placement of copy and what can be cropped. A poster seen in passing, a full-screen presentation and an image beside dense body text need different scale and detail. Check the result at both the real viewing size and the smallest meaningful derivative.

## Direct an illustration

1. **Assign a job.** An instructional diagram needs accurate relationships and visible steps. A product illustration needs honest representation. An editorial image can give an abstract idea a subject and mood. Decoration can establish atmosphere, but should not resemble evidence or fight with content.
2. **Choose the subject and treatment.** Specify what must be accurate, the focal subject, framing, perspective or degree of abstraction, light or mark-making, color roles, and the detail that must survive at the smallest size. A single purposeful analogy is usually stronger than an assortment of visual metaphors.
3. **Create a useful family.** For a series, carry rules for geometry, line weight, light, crop, scale or texture across the set. Vary composition where the subject needs it. Place a sample beside the real typography and in the actual crop before expanding it.
4. **Use the right production method.** Prefer editable vectors for marks, icons and clear line work; use raster, photography or generated images for richer material when appropriate. Use [generative work](GenerativeWork.md) when the process itself creates the artwork. Choose a capable tool if the brief calls for one. Set critical lettering and labels separately when the image method cannot render them reliably.
5. **Inspect the actual artwork.** Check subject accuracy, legible silhouette, clipping, awkward joins, lighting or anatomy when relevant, consistent treatment and export transparency. Give meaningful imagery an appropriate text alternative. Test a small mark or icon at its actual size and, when needed, with a single color.

For example, an installation guide can use a simplified side view and directional marks to make orientation unmistakable. The corresponding announcement might use a closer crop and fuller texture for atmosphere. Preserve the product's recognizable geometry in both.

## Design a sequence for a presentation

Name the audience's decision or question and organize the argument around it. Give each slide a clear job: orient, explain, compare, show evidence, or request a decision. Put evidence where it answers the claim, and move supporting detail to notes or an appendix when appropriate. Use repeated layout where comparison benefits; change scale or structure at a meaningful transition.

Compose for presentation or reading as requested. Check chart labels and units, type at the expected viewing distance, image quality, content order, repeated elements and speaker notes. Inspect the exported deck or PDF, including dense and sparse slides, as well as the editable file when one is requested. For timed motion or transitions, use [motion and gestures](MotionAndGestures.md).

## Produce and verify

For print, confirm finished size, bleed, safe margins, resolution, color handling and supplier requirements. For digital, check file weight, target dimensions, density, crops, background and transparency. For standalone visual web artifacts, open the actual delivered file or preview in its intended environment; verify external asset or offline assumptions when relevant. Keep editable source and separately reusable assets where needed. Give [Critique](../../critique/SKILL.md) the final export, brief and viewing conditions.
