# Visual language

Use when choosing or refining the visible and temporal qualities of a design. Work within an established brand or approved visual direction where one exists.

## Typography

- Choose type according to the task: reading comfort, character coverage, information density and tone. Verify the actual weights, scripts and licensing are available in the target environment.
- Assign distinct roles to titles, supporting text, labels and data. Establish hierarchy through a deliberate combination of size, weight, case, line height and spacing; use the existing type scale when it serves the project.
- Test real and unusually long content. Avoid clipped labels, accidental tiny copy, unreadably wide paragraphs and fixed-height text containers. Check zoom and text enlargement; preserve meaningful reading order.
- Use tabular figures where alignment helps compare numbers; do not make technical-looking type a substitute for legibility.

## Color and contrast

- Define what each color does: page and surface, primary and secondary text, interactive emphasis, borders and status. Use a controlled set of roles instead of assigning unrelated colors to individual elements.
- Compare foreground and background as pairs in all supported themes and states. Evaluate text, focus indicators, borders and visualized data against applicable accessibility criteria; do not assume a palette is accessible because individual colors look attractive.
- Use an additional cue such as text, shape or position when color communicates state. Check selected, disabled, error and success states on their actual surfaces, not only in an isolated swatch.
- Preserve explicitly chosen palette and contrast requirements. Avoid forcing a house palette or treating any hue or aesthetic as inherently wrong.

## Space, alignment and depth

- Make spacing express relationships: within a control, between related items, and between distinct groups. Reuse local spacing values where they fit; establish a small scale only when it helps consistency.
- Choose deliberate alignment edges and content widths. Let reading measure, the visual weight of images and the length of controls determine proportions rather than applying one grid to every surface.
- Use borders, shadows and layering to show separation or depth when needed. Check how stacking, clipping and overlays behave at different sizes; decoration should not be mistaken for an actionable control.

## Imagery and iconography

- Prefer authentic assets appropriate to the content. Know whether an image is real product material, user-supplied, licensed, generated or a placeholder; don't present a stand-in as evidence of a real customer or feature.
- Match crop, subject, scale and color treatment to the surrounding content. Reserve dimensions to avoid layout movement, and provide alternatives for meaningful imagery.
- Keep icons recognizable at their rendered size and visually consistent in weight and geometry. Pair ambiguous icons with text; name icon-only controls accessibly.

## Motion

Use movement to explain a change, preserve spatial context or add a deliberate expressive moment. Make duration and timing fit the interaction; ensure the final state does not depend on an animation completing. Respect reduced-motion settings, provide a way to pause persistent motion when required, and check performance on the target device. Avoid moving or flashing content that obscures reading, triggers discomfort or blocks input.

## Optional style examples

- **Soft editorial:** Lead with type and generous spacing. An asymmetric split can pair a dominant story with supporting imagery; stack it in reading order at narrow widths. Subtle depth can clarify layers and brief motion can orient people after a change. Keep controls evident and usable without hover or motion.
- **Industrial or Swiss print:** Use a consistent modular grid, strong type contrast and visible rules for genuinely separate groups. Use aligned figures and explicit units for real data; keep grain, halftone or scanline effects away from text and controls. Maintain contrast and distinguish states through labels or shape as well as color.

Use an example only if it suits the content and agreed direction. Translate its principles into the existing type, tokens, responsive behavior and platform conventions.
