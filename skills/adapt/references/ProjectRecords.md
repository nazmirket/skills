# Find evidence in project records

Start from the project question and the authorized collection. Use an existing contents page, file list, date, or domain term to narrow the candidate records. Include an older name or synonym when the first search misses. Check which version, period, population and unit each candidate describes.

- **Text and Markdown.** Search for the relevant term and read its section, including qualifications and any link to a newer decision.
- **PDF.** Find the heading or phrase in page-aware text, then inspect the original page when a column, table, footnote, or scan carries the answer. A failed text search on an image-only page leaves that page unchecked. Cite the document version, page and heading. Distinguish the printed page number from the viewer's index when they differ.
- **Workbook.** Inspect sheet names and headers, then locate the relevant rows and cells. Verify their dates, units and filters. When a value comes from a formula, check its inputs and whether its displayed result is current before relying on it. Cite the workbook version, sheet and range. Keep the source workbook intact.

For example, a team asks why its May report shows 46 active depots when the operating policy lists 48. The April policy on page 6 defines the 48 *eligible* depots. In the May workbook, `Roster!B2:B49` contains 46 active and two paused statuses. Report both counts with their definitions, dates and locations. If both records claim to count active depots for May, identify the conflicting entries and the missing reconciliation or owner decision. A newer modification date alone cannot settle which definition governs.
