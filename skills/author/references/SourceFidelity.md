# Check a conversion against its source

For a format change, establish what must carry over, including table relationships, figures, units, qualifications, required quotations and links. For a summary, identify which claims the destination will actually make and which details the reader needs to interpret them. Keep the original available for comparison through the final export.

| Source | Where extraction can mislead | Check against the original |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PDF or scan | Columns interleave, a minus sign disappears, or a footnote detaches from its value. | Inspect the rendered page. Trace a consequential number through its row, column, unit and note to its use in the draft. |
| Word document | Merged cells lose their spanning header, tracked changes expose two versions, or a quotation loses an attribution. | Inspect the intended revision and its rendered table or quoted passage. Resolve an ambiguous revision before attributing it. |
| Web article | Menus enter the body, a chart caption drops out, or relative links change target. | Compare the article body and captions in context. Open consequential links from the delivered format. |

For example, page 8 of a source report lists 4.2 under “Savings (€ thousands)” with a note excluding freight. A brief using the number should say “€4,200 in savings, excluding freight.” Check the wording in the delivered brief. Compare any quotation requested verbatim with the original passage as well. If a scanned chart on page 9 is unreadable, identify that specific gap and leave estimates from the chart out of the brief. State which consequential details you reconciled and any coverage that remains partial.
