# Author a design brief

Use when the intended effect, content, boundaries or approval need a shared record. Start from the request, useful project material and evidence already available. The human sets the intended effect and what the identity must preserve; Design chooses the composition and execution inside those bounds.

A working brief should answer:

- **Who and why:** audience, situation, primary task or feeling, and why the work is needed now.
- **What leads:** the real content, material, action or message the design should prioritize. Separate approved content and assets from examples, mood references and placeholders.
- **What must hold:** medium, target views or formats, established identity, access, working behavior, relevant devices and other constraints. State what could change, what must survive, and who decides open product choices.
- **What to deliver and check:** the direction, concept image, artifact or implementation; representative conditions; and the decision or acceptance evidence it should supply.

Write it tightly enough to reject a plausible but wrong direction. Reuse prior agreement; ask only about choices that materially change the result. For an interface concept image, get explicit agreement on the brief before generation and state separately whether it authorizes implementation.

Deliver the brief with open choices identified. A brief-only request stops there; approval of a brief grants only the authority it records.
