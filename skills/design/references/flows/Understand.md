# Understand the design problem

Use when the design ask is vague, the audience's job is uncertain, or the symptom may hide a different problem. Read the brief and relevant evidence. Use [Collect Evidence](CollectEvidence.md) only where the missing observation could change your interpretation.

1. Name who is trying to do or understand what, in which situation. Trace the entry, moment of uncertainty, action and feedback; use [Journey](../concepts/Journey.md) when the problem spans several steps or channels.
2. Separate the observed friction from your explanation and from a requested solution. A request to make a button louder could reflect unclear copy, a weak hierarchy, or an action that is unavailable at the point of need. Establish which view or state supports the diagnosis.
3. Identify what already works, which identity cues and behavior should survive, and what remains undecided. Compare a supplied example on the same task when it can resolve a question. Name the useful relationship, such as an explanation placed beside the choice it affects.
4. State the problem and the visible change that would address it: “For [person in situation], [observed issue] gets in the way of [job]. A successful result lets them [observable outcome], while preserving [constraint].” Label assumptions and ask about unresolved choices that would change the result.

Return the problem, evidence, non-negotiable constraints and the next decision. If understanding is the requested result, stop there.
