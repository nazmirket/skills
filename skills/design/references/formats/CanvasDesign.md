# Canvas design

Use for a composed static artifact such as a poster, cover, print piece, or visual handout. The format describes the finished surface and its handoff, regardless of the tool used. Use [Illustration Design](IllustrationDesign.md) when the image itself is the result, [Banner Design](BannerDesign.md) when placement crops or mounting drive the design, or [Presentation Design](PresentationDesign.md) when the sequence across slides is the result.

## Compose for the destination

Set the finished size, aspect ratio, viewing distance, target medium and delivery formats. For print, use the supplier's actual size, trim, bleed, safe area, resolution and color requirements. For digital, verify the destination's crop, target dimensions and background. Identify the copy, assets and facts that must remain intact.

Use [Composition](../concepts/Composition.md) and [Typography](../concepts/Typography.md) to establish the order a viewer should see: a focal subject or message, meaningful support, then details. Build a representative piece in the actual proportions. Keep required type and marks editable when practical; a full-canvas image can make small wording, alignment or reuse harder to correct. Treat decorative material as part of the same visual direction.

## Inspect and deliver the export

Open the actual export at intended use size. Also inspect the derivative that presents the biggest risk, such as a small thumbnail, a tight platform crop or a print cut line. Check image quality, visible bounds, readable wording, fine detail, background and asset links. For example, artwork that extends through bleed should not put essential text outside the safe area. Report a screen preview as such if physical reproduction has not been checked.

Deliver the agreed static exports and editable source or assets when requested. Include the size and relevant export settings. If the piece will be shared as an image, supply the meaningful wording or an accessible alternative where needed.
