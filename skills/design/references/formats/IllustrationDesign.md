# Illustration design

Use when the deliverable is an illustration or a family of illustrations. Use [Art](../concepts/Art.md) for shared art direction, [Vector Design](VectorDesign.md) when editable paths or tracing are the result, and [Canvas Design](CanvasDesign.md) when the illustration is one part of a composed piece with type and other material.

## Set the subject and visual rules

Name the illustration's job: explain a process, depict a real subject, give an idea a form, or supply atmosphere. Identify the intended placement, crops, minimum useful size, and what must remain factual or recognizable. Keep reference provenance and permitted use traceable. A stylized product image can simplify texture; it must not silently change features that the image is meant to explain.

Start with a readable silhouette and a hierarchy of shapes, then choose a treatment for line, color, material or texture that serves the job. Before making a family, prove one representative piece where it will actually appear, alongside the real type or content. Keep a few rules stable across the set, such as projection, line weight, light or palette roles. Vary pose, viewpoint and density when the subject calls for it.

## Check the artwork in its placement

When exploring silhouette and depth in a restricted palette, use the [ukiyo-e woodblock specimen](../styles/UkiyoE.md) to compare two compositions of the actual subject.

Inspect the final image at intended size and the smallest or tightest requested crop. Check the subject or sequence survives, contrast works against actual backgrounds, and fine details, edges or textures still read. Check labels separately when precision matters. For a family, compare a simple and a difficult subject; repeat the rules, not the same composition.

Return the agreed assets and editable sources where requested, plus any variants for crop, background or reproduction. Check dimensions, transparency and legibility in the actual export. For informative artwork, supply an appropriate text alternative or state where it belongs; note unsupported uses or fidelity limits.
