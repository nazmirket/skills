# Logo design

Use for a wordmark, symbol, monogram, emblem, or set of logo lockups. Use [Brand](../concepts/Brand.md) for the larger identity and [Icons](../concepts/Icons.md) for functional interface symbols. Establish whether the request is for directions, a revision to a selected mark, or production files.

## Define the mark's job

Identify the exact name and spelling, audience, desired association, and identity cues that must survive. Ask where the mark must work: an app icon, a narrow header, a large sign, or a particular print process can call for different versions. Note permitted assets and references through [Collect Evidence](../flows/CollectEvidence.md).

Choose a mark type for the job. A wordmark gives the name priority; a symbol travels where the name cannot fit; a lockup makes their relationship repeatable. When direction is open, compare genuinely different ideas in equivalent placements so color or mockup styling does not decide for you. Start in one color when reproduction and shape are the main questions. Check visual balance, spacing and counters at real size; aligned coordinates alone do not establish optical balance. Use [Vector](../concepts/Vector.md) for editable construction and [Typography](../concepts/Typography.md) for lettering decisions.

## Prove recognition and reproduction

| Case | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Smallest real placement | The silhouette reads, internal spaces do not close, and important lettering survives. Simplify the small version if scaling the master loses the idea. |
| One color and reversed | Recognition survives without the palette; thin details, negative space, and apparent weight hold on both grounds. |
| Real context | The lockup fits surrounding type and the actual background; the mark is recognizable alongside neighboring marks without inheriting another's distinctive form. |

A similarity check can flag visual confusion. Report only what was compared and leave legal clearance unverified.

## Hand over the requested result

For concept work, deliver the agreed comparisons and stop at selection. For production, open the actual exports and check bounds, transparency, clipping and letterforms. Provide the editable master and the approved versions for their intended uses, such as horizontal, stacked, symbol-only, or reversed. Name files by use and include minimum size or clear-space guidance where helpful. Keep editable lettering and any outlined delivery copy distinct, and identify any required fonts or assets.

Use [Figma](../tools/Figma.md) or [OpenPencil](../tools/OpenPencil.md) when the source or requested editable master uses that editor.
