# Presentation design

Use for a talk, pitch, lesson, or deck that people read on their own. The same format can ship as an editable deck, PDF, or browser presentation. Identify the audience, the decision or takeaway, the viewing conditions, and which output they need to present, read, or edit.

## Build the argument

- **Live talk:** Give each slide a clear job at speaking pace. Keep the claim, image or comparison legible from the expected distance; put explanation in speaker notes or an appendix when useful.
- **Reading deck:** Include enough context to make claims and figures understandable without a speaker. Use short takeaways, titles that establish order, and explicit evidence with units and sources.

Sequence what the audience needs to know before they can follow the next idea. Use a recurring layout for repeated comparisons and change it when the story does. For an open direction, compare approaches on the same real material, including a demanding slide before turning one attractive opener into a system. Keep dense slides readable: if a section does not fit at the intended stage and scale, split the material or move supporting detail. Use [Composition](../concepts/Composition.md) and [Typography](../concepts/Typography.md) for craft decisions. Use [Diagram Design](DiagramDesign.md) for relationships that require a diagram.

## Verify the actual handoff

Open the format the audience will receive. Inspect an opener, a dense slide and any other slide that introduces a new layout or feature. Check viewing size, clipping and overlap, text wrapping, image and chart readability, labels, footnotes and the order that matters. Exercise navigation, media, links and timing if they are part of delivery. For example, a figure that fits in the editor might reflow with substituted fonts on the presentation machine; check the exported file or mark that environment unverified.

Keep slide titles, reading order and text alternatives useful in the delivered format. [PowerPoint's accessibility guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities) is relevant when the deliverable is a PowerPoint file. Give the requested deck and editable source, with notes, assets, and font or playback dependencies as applicable. Check each requested export and state what could not be verified.
