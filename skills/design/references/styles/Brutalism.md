# Brutalism

A direct, exposed visual language can fit cultural work, editorial experiments or a project that wants its construction to be visible.

## Build the visual language

Build with a blunt structural grid, visible boundaries, unvarnished imagery, and a limited typographic system. Let scale and raw material do the expressive work. Treat asymmetry or an abrupt crop as a deliberate decision about what should lead.

## Avoid the cliché

Distinguish it from Neo-Brutalism: bluntness and exposed structure can work without bright fills, chunky shadows or playful stickers. Avoid neglecting hierarchy, readable text, or the location of real controls.

## Check it in use

Read the main action or headline at actual size. Check whether the rawness still feels intentional when ordinary content replaces the hero.

The Barbican’s rough concrete finish makes the work of forming the surface visible. Inspect how that texture reads at building scale. For a page or poster, choose one construction detail to expose and check that the labels still stand apart from it. [Barbican architecture archive](https://www.barbican.org.uk/s/barbicanfacts)
