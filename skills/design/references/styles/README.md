# Design styles

Choose a treatment when it helps the audience recognize, understand or act on the subject. Begin with the project's approved identity, the actual content and the viewing conditions. A named style can clarify a decision. An existing product may already have all the visual language it needs.

## Choose a direction

Take one representative piece of material. Decide what leads, how the reader moves through it, where density changes and what carries identity. Specify a type relationship, a color role, an image or mark-making rule and a surface treatment that support those decisions. Use motion only where its timing explains a change or serves the brief.

For example, a sound archive could open on a field recording with a caption and transcript for a listening audience. A researcher identifying a recording needs comparable rows with duration, date, rights status and a playable excerpt. The same archive mark and recording can support either direction. Try both with the same long title and unavailable audio. Judge whether listeners can hear the piece or researchers can identify the right record. A change of palette alone would leave that choice unresolved.

Use the selected guide on one demanding section before spreading the treatment. Check a long label, a quiet region, an important state and the narrow or small-format reading. An expressive introduction can give way to calmer controls, tables or instructions. Preserve familiar cues for links, focus and status.
