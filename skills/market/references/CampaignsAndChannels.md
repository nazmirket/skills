# Campaigns and channels

Use for a campaign, go-to-market communication, or distribution plan. Write the outcome as a change among a defined audience, not a list of posts. Check product readiness with Ship before committing to availability, eligibility, dates or reach.

| Decision | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Audience and offer | Segment, trigger, promise, supporting proof, and intended action |
| Channel fit | Where that audience already pays attention, why the message fits, delivery constraints and cost |
| Sequence | What primes, announces and follows up; destination, owner, dependency, and review status for each touch |
| Test and spend | Smallest useful pilot, baseline, attribution limits, decision window, and stop or scale rule |
| Readiness | Verified destination, permissions, audience exclusions, response owner, and support plan |

Compare owned channels, external communities or partners, and paid reach individually; one may be enough. Adapt the same promise to each format instead of posting the same message everywhere. For partner or sales handoff, include approved claims, common objections, collateral, and which questions need an owner rather than an improvised answer.

Match distribution to the buying path. For self-service, trace a channel link through signup to first value. For an assisted sale, define what qualifies a lead, who accepts it, response timing and how rejected leads return to the correct owner. For a partner pilot, agree on audience overlap, permissions, division of work and how both parties will count the same outcome. Use [Acquisition economics](AcquisitionEconomics.md) when budget allocation is the decision.

Check the destination, tracking, disclosure, campaign dates, link visibility and approved terms using a preview or test recipient where authorized. Prepare the assets or calendar within scope; sending, publishing, outreach, and spend require their own authority.

## Find the first customers

Start with a group the founder knows well enough to recognize a recurring workaround. Identify who does the work, who could buy, where a conversation is welcome, and which part of the offer the team can already deliver. Give the founder one draft they could use with a relevant buyer.

For example, a founder at a wholesale bakers' meetup hears owners describe copying café orders from messages into packing sheets. Assume the team has approved a $30 trial for one delivery run. The founder could use this draft with an owner who described doing that work.

> You mentioned retyping café orders on delivery mornings. For $30, I can turn one run's item counts into a packing list grouped by stop. Would you like to see a sample before deciding?

For each authorized conversation, record the furthest action the buyer took, their stated reason and the next question. Suppose one owner declines because orders already arrive in a standard form, another views a sample, and a third pays for a run and orders again the following week. The first response suggests testing the offer with bakeries that take orders in mixed formats. The second leaves purchase intent open. Ask the returning buyer what made a second run worthwhile. Use those conversations to refine whom to approach next. Take a proposed service or price change to Ship for a recommendation and to the human for a decision.
