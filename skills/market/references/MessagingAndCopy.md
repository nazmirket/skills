# Messaging and copy

Begin with one audience, one decision, and where the message lands. Describe what the reader already knows, what just brought them there, what might stop them, and what they should be able to do next. Use the approved positioning and brand voice; don't invent an offer to fix weak copy.

- Make a message hierarchy: outcome first, how it works where it earns understanding, proof next to the claim, and friction addressed before the relevant action. A comparison page may need criteria early; a short ad usually earns a visit rather than explaining the entire product.
- Make the primary action specific and match its destination. “See a sample report” should open a sample. Do not imply free access or immediate results when billing, approval, or setup is required.
- Maintain a claim-to-evidence list for material assertions, with scope and exclusions. Use exact supported details in place of superlatives. For customer proof, distinguish an attributed result from a representative result and confirm permission before publication.
- Edit in two passes: first check that the message, proof and action form an honest argument; then remove repeated ideas, abstractions, jargon and awkward rhythm. Recheck truncation, links, localized text, and tier, date or currency details where the message will appear.

Return usable copy with alternatives only when they represent materially different messages. Leave unsupported claims out or mark what evidence would make them publishable. Pass visual hierarchy to Design when the presentation also needs work.
