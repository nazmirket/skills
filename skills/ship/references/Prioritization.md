# Prioritization and roadmaps

Start with the outcome, the decision window, and genuine constraints: obligations, capacity, dependencies or a time-sensitive opportunity. Classify proposals by the problem they solve and separate contractual or safety commitments from bets.

When the outcome is already set, use it to compare opportunities here. Open [Product Strategy](ProductStrategy.md) only if the task also calls for choosing or reconsidering the product direction, target segment, or outcome. Choosing a near-term bet within the agreed outcome stays in Prioritization.

1. Compare opportunities before individual features. Ask what improves for whom, over what horizon, and how strongly evidence supports that expectation. Distinguish customer frequency, severity and reach; the loudest request need not be the most common or costly.
2. Compare the smallest viable response, deferral and alternatives. Consider effort, reversibility, support burden, dependencies and cost of delay. Use coarse scores only where they illuminate disagreements. Show confidence, ranges and sensitivity; avoid arithmetic that launders estimates into facts.
3. Convert top items to outcomes with a baseline or plan to obtain one, measure, owner, confidence and next check. Name commitments versus hypotheses. Use precision for true deadlines; keep later work conditional rather than turning guesses into dates.
4. Record what moved and why, what is deliberately out, and what new evidence would reorder it. Keep the roadmap readable for its intended audience. Give approved work to Plan for dependencies and implementation sequencing.

Example: replace “build saved filters” with “reduce repeated setup for returning analysts; compare saved filters and restoring the last view.” An outcome gives room to choose a simpler implementation.

When the top two bets are close, put them in the same unit and horizon. For example, eliminating three minutes of setup in 100 sessions a week could recover five hours at full adoption. A second option saving one minute in 180 weekly sessions could recover three hours. The first drops behind if fewer than 60% of its eligible sessions benefit and the second reaches all of its sessions. Validate that uncertain reach before letting the estimates determine the order. Record engineering effort and ongoing cost alongside the benefit.
