# Product strategy

Express direction as a choice about whom to serve, what outcome to improve, and what the team will forgo. Start with the current strategy and performance; don't infer agreement from a roadmap or from a competitor's move.

- Identify the initial segment by situation and need, including buyers and users when they differ. Describe what they currently do, switching costs, and why a change matters now. Mark market size, value or timing as assumptions when evidence is thin.
- Compare viable choices, including preserving the current direction. For each, name the outcome, capability required, relative cost and opportunity cost, plausible advantage, and what would make the choice wrong. If an option only wins under optimistic assumptions, make them visible.
- State the choice as a strategic bet: “Serve [segment] by improving [outcome] through [approach], while deferring [tradeoff].” Add evidence, owner, success signal, and a condition that would cause revisit or exit.
- Separate stable direction from current experiments and delivery promises. Test the riskiest assumption with [Discovery](Discovery.md) before turning a broad direction into an expensive commitment.

Return a decision brief, including the recommendation and the strongest case against it. Leave the decision to the human. Use Market once the job is to communicate the resulting value proposition externally.

## Size a reachable opportunity

Choose one unit, geography, buyer and period. Estimate eligible accounts from observable constraints, then apply the share reachable through actual distribution, sales and onboarding capacity. Use price or contribution per account in consistent units. Record overlap, data dates, ranges and which assumption dominates the estimate. Compare with an independent count where available.

For example, 600 eligible teams at an assumed $1,200 a year imply a $720,000 annual revenue ceiling. With capacity to onboard 20 teams next quarter, filling every slot would add at most $24,000 in annual recurring revenue at quarter end under those assumptions. To forecast cash received or recognized revenue for the quarter, account for acquisition timing, terms and losses.
